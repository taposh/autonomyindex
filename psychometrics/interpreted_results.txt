## 2. Missingness Model
**Why we do it**: To check if missingness is systematic (e.g., related to specific domains). If missingness is non-random, it can bias the reliability and agreement results.
**What the results say**: Because LLMs had 0% item N/A, there is no item-level missingness to model. Call-level failures exist but are handled as non-responses.

## 3-5. Variance Components, Reliability, Decision Study

## 6. Ordinal Alpha
**Why we do it**: Standard Cronbach's alpha underestimates reliability on ordinal (Likert) scales. Polychoric-based ordinal alpha correctly estimates internal consistency.
  July LLM: Ordinal α = 0.978, Pearson-based α = 0.968
  Human: Ordinal α = 0.971, Pearson-based α = 0.959
**What the results say**: Both LLMs and humans exhibit excellent internal consistency (alpha > 0.95), meaning items within the index reliably measure the same constructs.

## 8. Tier-Weighted Correlations
**Why we do it**: To evaluate how well LLM scores correlate with the human consensus AI, giving more weight to vignettes with more human raters.
  July LLM: Unweighted r = 0.781, Rater-weighted r = 0.763
**What the results say**: July LLMs strongly and positively correlate with human scores (weighted r ~ 0.76).

## 9. Mixed Difference Model (Bias Model)
**Why we do it**: To estimate the systematic scoring gap (bias) between humans and LLMs while accounting for vignette clustering.
**What the results say**: Highlights the overall magnitude and direction of the human-LLM gap (whether LLMs over-score or under-score compared to humans).

## 10. Kenward-Roger F-Test
**Why we do it**: Mixed models assume infinite degrees of freedom for the Wald test. Kenward-Roger corrects this for small samples (like 50 vignettes) to accurately test if the bias is non-zero.
**What the results say**: Evaluates statistical significance of the bias with adjusted degrees of freedom.

## 11. Domain-by-Source Joint Test
**Why we do it**: To see if the human-LLM gap varies significantly across the 4 Autonomy Index domains (VA, FU, RD, IA).
  Joint Wald χ²(3) = 2.267   p = 0.5189
**What the results say**: A p > 0.05 indicates the human-LLM gap is uniform across domains, meaning the LLMs do not behave significantly differently on one domain versus another.

## 12. Bland-Altman
**Why we do it**: To visualize the agreement between human and LLM scores, checking for proportional bias and displaying 95% Limits of Agreement.
  July LLM: Bias(H−L) = 11.01, 95% LoA = [-21.85, 43.88]
**What the results say**: Provides a practical range within which 95% of the differences between human and LLM scores fall.

## 13. Holm-Bonferroni Correction
**Why we do it**: To perform item-by-item comparisons between humans and LLMs, correcting for the family-wise error rate of running 15 simultaneous tests.
**What the results say**: Identifies specific rubric items where the LLM's scoring significantly diverges from humans.
